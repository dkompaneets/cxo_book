# Ending variant 27.3 — why do they keep going

[book/variants/chapter-27.3.md](../book/variants/chapter-27.3.md). Supersedes 27.2. The pub is
27.1's through *he was terribly pleased about it* — the reasoning for that part is in
[ending-variant-27.1.md](ending-variant-27.1.md). Everything after is new. No Monday. No Fenella.

## The question

*Why do they keep going?* — asked twice by the author and not answered by 27.1 or 27.2, which
answered it with Alex's eruption (true for Alex, buried in a technical passage, not the book's
answer).

The book already holds two answers, one per father figure, the same sentence with opposite
outcomes:

- **Michael, ch25:** *I was good at it. If I'd been worse at it I'd have been home.* He kept going
  as repayment — the sticker on the television, a promise a boy made. A debt. It killed him.
- **Marek, ch24:** *I did it because it was in front of me and there was nobody else to do it. I did
  not think about it.* Not a debt. *It is a thing that is given. You spend it.* The only person in
  the book who is not in the vise.

They keep going because they think they owe it. Kate to Harriet, whom she chose because she would
be harder on her. Nik to Chennai and the school and forty *how was work*s a week. Alex to the
bakery. None of them was ever charged. That is the vise: the next thing in front of you, done as
repayment of a debt nobody sent.

The book is finally for Marek's answer. Nobody at the table can give it.

## What 27.3 does

### Claire asks it

She is the only person entitled to. Straight after the telephone, without weight, *the way you ask
when the trains are*: *He told Danny what it cost. I've watched it cost Danny. I'm sitting here
with three more of you. Monday morning. Why.*

### Three answers, four registers

- **Kate:** *Because I can see it coming. I can see it before anyone else in the building, and if
  I'm not there when it lands, it lands.* Ch5's belief — anything she is not holding will be
  dropped — said out loud as a reason. She hears it and lets it stand.
- **Nik:** the version he gives (school, knee, boiler) and, asked for the truer one, *It's the only
  thing anyone's ever paid me for. I'm forty-two. I don't know what I'd be on a Tuesday.* Michael's
  empty Wednesday afternoon, one generation down.
- **Alex:** can't. *It's the thing. Not the company. The thing.* His is the only answer at the
  table that is not a debt, and it will not come up. His peak: the flow gone.
- **Michael**, last, from the dead, via Danny: *I was good at it. If I'd been worse at it I'd have
  been home.* — *Danny thought it was a joke.* His jokes always land; even the one that wasn't.

Then the fortnight: Danny was different for about two weeks after the step, then he wasn't. *I
don't blame him. It's his job.* The most expensive thing Michael ever did, priced.

### Two codas, two phones

The whole chapter has been phones that did not ring: Michael's on the table from August, the
voicemail Alex never returned, the group Nik never wrote in.

**Bow.** Alex rings his father at twenty-five to eleven with nothing to say. *I haven't got
anything. I just rang.* Ten seconds, which on that line is nothing at all. Marek: *OK. Your mother
is asleep. I am not. So.* — and the section ends on the invitation without the word. This is the
only spent inheritance in the book. Forty seconds, no content, no *it's fine, Papa*, nothing
fixed. Marek gets the last line of dialogue because he is the one who answered the question.

**Watford.** Same hour. Danny on the phone in the kitchen — the thumbs, a thread with nine people
on it. Claire in the door with her coat on. He smiles and holds up one finger. She waits. Sam on
the stairs in pyjamas, awake because of a plan, watches his father hold up the finger and his
mother wait. *Then he went back up.*

Ch25, Claire: *Sam thinks it's normal that grandparents don't come to things, and I never
corrected it.* Now he learns the finger. The vise, made of each other, closing on a six-year-old
who is Michael's grandson, eleven minutes on a step notwithstanding. Claire knows everything and
waits anyway — self-tightened, with a face.

### Why this order

Bow before Watford. The grace note, then the cut. Ending on Marek would be consolation; ending on
Sam is the vise. Both are on the page, and the book does not say which weighs more.

### The title

Everything now happens on Friday. *Friday* is right again.

## Aftertaste

Three people said why, out loud, to a dead man's daughter, and none of the reasons was good enough,
and they are going in on Monday. One of them rang his father for nothing. And a six-year-old in
Watford learned what a raised finger means. The reader has heard their own answer at that table
and watched where it goes.
