# Chapter ranking

Ranked by how alive the chapter is on the page: whether the register is audible without the names, whether the turn is enacted in a scene rather than reported, whether the chapter ends on an image, and what it costs the character. Structural load is noted but not scored; a chapter can be indispensable and still be the flattest thing in the book.

Chapters 1 and 2 are the calibration set the register harness was tuned on and were left as they were; chapter 3's first half was thinned after a read-aloud. Chapters 7, 12, 15, 19, 22 and 23 are the ones the reference files name as working and got nothing beyond continuity fixes. Chapters 9, 18 and 20 were lifted in a later pass whose only tool was the cut: every sentence that told the reader what the scene had just done came out, and the ensemble chapter got the register markers it was missing.

## The ten below nine, and what each would take

These are the changes that would move each chapter, and why they were not made in a pass whose rule was not to break what works.

- **10.** Its movement is ch1's: she nearly says it and does not. A 9 needs an event of its own, and the only candidate is Nik seeing the deflection, which reverses what the outline decided: visible to the reader, not to him.
- **11.** The set-piece about what nobody said is the chapter's design. A 9 would put the four registers in one exchange, colliding, instead of in four separate sections around the extractor.
- **24.** The negotiation of terms runs in summary where the rest of the book gives a scene. A 9 plays it out under the clock Alex cannot stop noticing, which is a rewrite of the second half.
- **21.** Four endings, each right and each short. A 9 gives one of them full length, and the candidate is Marek in Terminal 2.
- **4.** Length only. A 9 loses two hundred words from the commute and the trophy cabinet, and both passages are in register and doing work.
- **1.** Untouched as the calibration chapter. The tell in it, and the one thought the narrator explains, would be a two-line edit if the chapter is ever reopened.
- **8.** The four questions are the plot. A 9 has Michael pay for the lecture inside the scene beyond the card and the face: cut question two and let Kate ask him one that he declines. That changes what ch14 can do.
- **14.** Two speeches in two chairs. A 9 needs a third presence, or Kate's clock stopping during his confession, and the stopped clock is her peak's device and would spend it before ch15.
- **5.** The form. A 9 cuts two of the four escalations to a paragraph, and every one of the four plants something used later: the third review layer, the lost idea, the recital, the card balance he cannot type.
- **6.** Built not to be under pressure. A 9 would need pressure, and the chapter is the eighteen minutes of relief before Wilf.
