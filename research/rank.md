# Chapter ranking

Ranked by how alive the chapter is on the page: whether the register is audible without the names, whether the turn is enacted in a scene rather than reported, whether the chapter ends on an image, and what it costs the character. Structural load is noted but not scored; a chapter can be indispensable and still be the flattest thing in the book.

## The bottom five, and what would lift them

- **10** would be stronger at two thirds the length. Cut the Peloton riff to a single sentence so that *That's nice* is the loudest thing on the page, and let *what is it you think you're protecting* land in the room upstairs with nothing after it but the answer.
- **6** is not asking to be lifted. It is the board being set and it should stay light. The one improvement is sensory: each arrival could carry that character's channel (Nik prices, Alex hears the plant, Michael smells the lobby, Kate times the queue) and nothing else.
- **16** should lose about a hundred and fifty words of gloss on the emptiness and keep the lift, the coin in a foreign currency, and the deleted messages to Dawn. What she feels is already enacted by what she deletes.
- **8** cannot lose the four questions; the book needs *since when*. It could give Michael one more thing that costs him inside the scene, so that the lecture is paid for on the page rather than by the Christmas card anecdote alone.
- **5** is the outline in prose. It would read less like a beat sheet if two of the four escalations were cut to a paragraph and the other two (Michael's laugh from outside, Nik unable to type the card balance) were allowed to be full scenes.
