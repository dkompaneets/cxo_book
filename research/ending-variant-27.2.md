# Ending variant 27.2 — the phone on the table

[book/variants/chapter-27.2.md](../book/variants/chapter-27.2.md). Supersedes 27.1. The pub is
27.1's, unchanged except for one addition; the ending is rebuilt as a single beat. The reasoning
for the pub — the telephone as the real cost, Claire in the fourth chair, the three failures, the
voicemail as a gift — is in [ending-variant-27.1.md](ending-variant-27.1.md) and still stands.

## Why 27.1 failed

- **Three people winning on Monday is not the vise.** It is continuation. The vise is a mechanism
  — real, self-tightened, made of each other — and it has to be seen closing on the page, on
  someone, by a force the reader can trace.
- **Michael was not on the last page.** The connection (*the day you stop, it stops*) lived in the
  reasoning doc. On the page it was a man doing a call well.
- **Diluted.** Three medium beats where one hard one was needed.

## What 27.2 does

### Claire adds the fortnight

Between the unfinished *he does a joke at the end so you can't—* and the telephone:

> "He did it to Danny, too. The night of the recital, on the step. Eleven minutes, and not one
> joke in it, which is the only time. And he was different for about a fortnight. Then he wasn't.
> He's on the phone at breakfast again. I don't blame him. It's his job."

The most expensive thing Michael ever did bought a fortnight. The vise, reported from inside the
family, as a fact. *I don't blame him. It's his job* — the pressure is real, and Claire is fair.

### One beat: Alex, Monday

Kate's and Nik's Monday sections are cut. They had their peaks in the pub.

1. **Room tone.** The grilles. Alex at the loud end. Fenella at the quiet end, thirty feet away,
   back to the room.
2. **Call nineteen.** Ben comes over the top and stops himself. Six seconds. The eruption — the
   memory-pressure answer, full length, delight. Why he keeps going, enacted.
3. **The follow-up is Fenella's.** The fail-closed question — the certificate she fixed by hand at
   twenty to four in July while he was in Riga telling Ben about silence. He opens his mouth,
   because he knows, and shuts it. *Fenella knows this better than I do.* Six seconds, a different
   six. *I'll write it up.* Ben moves on.
4. **He does the right thing.** Writes to her. *To her. Just her.* — the voicemail, enacted
   verbatim. *You, not me, not Ben.* Reads it twice and sends it once — the advisory sheet, ch25.
5. **The vise closes anyway.** Nothing back. She is thirty feet away, doing something else with
   the whole of her attention, the way he did. He does not go over. *She would tell him when she
   could* — Marek, ch24, the worst thing he is good at.
6. **The last line.** *He put his phone on the table where he could see it, and left it there.*

### Why this is the vise, shown

Fenella is under pressure (Arcus, the follow-up, being routed around), and she freezes, which is
what Alex did for four years. The company will answer it without her by Wednesday, and the reader
knows it. Alex did the right thing, and is now the one waiting, with twenty-one calls to go. Real.
Self-tightened. Made of each other. One turn, visible, in one room, with no inference required.

### Why Michael is connected

Three facts on the page, no narrator:

- *Just her* — the apology in the voicemail, which the reader heard in full in ch25 and again in
  the pub.
- *Read it twice and sent it once* — the single sheet he read twice and signed once.
- *The phone on the table* — Claire's sentence about her father, an hour earlier in story time.
  Now in a twenty-six-year-old's hand.

The reader watched a man wait for a phone and die. Now a young man puts his on the table. Nothing
to decode.

### Three phones

Danny's at breakfast. Michael's on the table from August. Alex's on the table from Monday. The
vise as an object.

## Aftertaste

Not *the show goes on*. A man who did the right thing, waiting, in his father's patience and a
dead man's habit, for a girl who is thirty feet away and will not come — because she is him. The
change is real. The vise is real. The reader holds both and the book does not say which weighs
more.

## Dependencies

As 27.1: ch26 loses its last four paragraphs so the death is not spent early; three illness
plants (ch11, ch19, ch20). Title stays *Friday*.
