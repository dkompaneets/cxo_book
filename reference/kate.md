# Kate Merrick — ostinato

The Soviet news intro. Brass, martial, relentless, no rubato — a repeating rhythmic figure
that never develops and never resolves.

## Authors

- **Hilary Mantel** — power-interiority. The ruthless close third; a mind that assesses
  everyone in the room instantly and files them.
- **Jenny Offill**, *Dept. of Speculation* — segmentation. White space, the timed fragment,
  the accumulating unlovely list.
- **Deborah Levy** — the flash-image dropped into abstraction and left unexplained.

## Prosody

- **Shortest sentences in the book.** Mean 12–14, against Michael's 25+.
- Verb-first and imperative. She thinks in commands.
- No semicolons. Full stops.
- Asyndeton — lists without conjunctions. The brass stabs.
- Almost no subordination; subordination implies leisure.
- Sentences that stop before their object, because there is not time to finish.
- **Timestamps are her bar line, not decoration.** Every scene runs on the clock. This is the
  instrument, and it already works: ch1's twelve-minute blocks and ch15's countdown are the
  two places the book sounds like her.

## The one modulation

Her body knows what her mind will not. The foot turning out at the kitchen island, the weight
on the ball of the foot, the shoulder coming in on the two and the four. This is the only
place her prose is permitted to slow down — one bar of something else, then back to brass.
Never explain it. She does not catch it until after it has happened, and often not then.

## At the peak

She loses the clock. The timestamps stop. A woman whose every scene has been scaffolded on
the time is suddenly in a passage with no time in it at all.

## In the current draft

Her strongest chapter is ch22 — playing the message back, Dawn's *"oh, Kate"*, handing the
crisis to Tom, Colin at the dance class, Dawn's eight years of Wednesdays. Ch15 is the
best-constructed chapter in the book and it is hers.

The problem to fix: ch10 repeats ch1's movement — she almost tells and doesn't — and ch16 is
the only chapter in the book with no joke in it anywhere.

Her chapters: 1, 8, 10, 14, 15, 16, 22. Sections in 5, 11, 21, 26, 27.

## Watch for

- The house voice reasserting itself. She is the character most often written in it, because
  she opens the book and the opening set the register.
- Explanatory warmth — ch16 and ch22 have both drifted into it.
- Making the ostinato decorative. If the timestamps could be deleted without loss, they are
  ornament and not structure.
