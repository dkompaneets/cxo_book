# Aleksander Wójcik — Norwegian rock

**Motorpsycho.** Long form, unannounced transitions, technically formidable and never showing
off; revered by the few who care and unknown to everyone else — which is Alex exactly. Two
thousand people watched the Seattle talk and nobody at his own company has asked what he
charges.

## The four movements

Every Alex chapter runs these, in order:

1. **Room tone.** Cold, spacious, precise. Short sentences with air around them, over a
   literal drone — air handling, fans, the train, the office at two in the morning. He is
   never in silence. *"The building's air handling doing its long slow animal breathing"*
   (ch2) is the bed; it should be under everything.
2. **The build.** The passage that appears to be going nowhere and turns out to have been
   assembling the whole time. Write the assembly, not the arrival.
3. **The eruption.** Long, dense, unstoppable and **joyful**. Pleasure, not catharsis — this
   is the happiest anyone gets in this book. Runs to 600 words if it needs to. Motorpsycho's
   permission: twelve minutes is a normal length.
4. **The return.** It stops. The room tone is still there. Nobody noticed anything happened.

## Authors

- **Per Petterson** (*Out Stealing Horses*) and **Karl Ove Knausgård** — the quiet.
  Flat-surfaced prose with enormous pressure underneath; physical and technical work described
  exactly. Knausgård specifically for the humiliation of a young man in a room who cannot
  speak. **Tarjei Vesaas** if it needs to be colder still.
- **Nicholson Baker**, *The Mezzanine* — the eruption. Nobody is better on the beauty of
  mechanism.

## Register

His prose sits **deliberately outside the anglophone social comedy** the other three operate
in. British idiom is the house rule; Alex is the exception. In the room, not of it — the son
of a man whose name has to be spelled *it's a W but it's a V*.

## At the peak

He loses the flow. The eruption does not come. He is left in room tone with nothing arriving,
and the reader — who has been given the eruption three or four times by then — feels the
absence as a physical fact.

## In the current draft

The biggest single missed opportunity in the book is **ch12**: it summarises his flow from
Michael's side instead of entering it. Split it — Michael's POV for the room, then hand the
prose to Alex and let the eruption run at full length and full speed. Michael's sixty per
cent comprehension becomes the reader's sixty per cent.

Ch2 is the weakest chapter in the book at least partly for the same reason: the seventy silent
minutes are described in retrospect, at a distance, in the house voice. Write them as the
seventy minutes.

Ch24 is the return, played quiet. He says it once, slowly, and the room tone is still going
underneath.

His chapters: 2, 9, 13 (with Nik), 17, 24. Sections in 5, 11, 21, 26. Present in 12.

## Watch for

- **Describing the dysfluency in fluent sentences.** This is the single clearest example of
  the whole disease: *"the words just aren't there, like reaching into a pocket"* is reported
  by a narrator who is having no such trouble. Enact it in the syntax or cut it.
- The quiet becoming mere stammering. It is not failure — it is cold, spacious and deliberate.
- The eruption written as suffering. It is delight.
