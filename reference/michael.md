# Michael Halloway — classical

Statement, development, return. His paragraphs recapitulate. When he approaches the true
thing the sentence modulates and then resolves back to the tonic anyway — that is the deep
breath rendered as syntax, and it means the withholding never has to be described.

## Authors

- **Kazuo Ishiguro**, *The Remains of the Day* — the architecture. Stevens is a man who
  optimised everything except the point, who cannot say the true thing, and who never once
  gets a vantage on himself. That is already the canon rule for Michael, written by somebody
  else forty years ago. Steal the qualifying opener and the professional euphemism doing
  emotional labour.
- **Alan Bennett** — the jokes. The deflating concrete noun at the end of a dignified
  sentence. *"The soul doesn't have to do the RAG report"* is already pure Bennett.
- **William Trevor** — the peaks. Drop the subordination entirely, go short and plain, and
  let it be terrible.

## Prosody

- Longest sentences in the book, by a clear margin. Mean 25+ against Kate's 12–14.
- **The only character who gets semicolons.**
- Subordination that resolves. Latinate where the others are Anglo-Saxon.
- Speaks in finished paragraphs. Nobody else in the book gets a finished paragraph.
- Never a fragment. Never a dash where a semicolon would serve.
- The comic register is dry institutional precision — but he no longer owns the book's
  comedy. That went to Nik. Michael's jokes are *load-bearing*: each one is a door he is
  choosing not to walk through.

## At the peak

He loses his subordination. The clauses stop arriving. What is left is short, plain and
unqualified — and because he has spent the whole book resolving every sentence, the reader
feels the floor go without being told.

## In the current draft

His strongest writing is ch19 — the seat at the recital, Terry's call, the laugh dying flat
across the carpet. That laugh is the only failed joke in the book and it should not be.

The problem to fix: through ch14 he asks questions, diagnoses the other three, and wants
nothing that costs him anything. He is a talking head with the best lines. Ch12 is his own
chapter and his role in it is to *watch*. Give him one thing he wants and cannot have,
planted by ch3, costing him something on the page well before ch19.

His chapters: 3, 12, 19, 25. Sections in 5, 11, 21. Present in 8, 14, 20.

## Watch for

- Wisdom delivery. Three consecutive scenes of Michael explaining life to somebody (8, 12,
  14) is the structural weakness of the whole front half.
- The periodic sentence becoming a mannerism. If a reader can name the device, pull back.
